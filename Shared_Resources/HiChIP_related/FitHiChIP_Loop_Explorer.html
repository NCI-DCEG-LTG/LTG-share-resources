What an arc means

A FitHiChIP arc is a statistically called interaction between two genomic bins, not a photograph of a physical loop.

What this viewer adds

It helps you find a gene or rsID, highlight its position, inspect counts and −log10(q) scores, and export the currently shown calls.

Important limit

Optional peak/signal tracks add ChIP-seq or ATAC-seq context, but without a .hic/.cool matrix this page still cannot show contact intensity. Visual presence/absence is not a differential-loop statistical test.

How to read the result

A short guide for non-specialist users.

  1. Search a gene, rsID, or region. Gene searches center on the gene; an rsID centers on its assembly-matched SNP and marks it with a yellow band.
  2. Navigate the shared tracks. Drag the amber ruler above the loops to select a region and zoom. Drag anywhere in the aligned tracks below the ruler to pan left or right. The mouse wheel scrolls the page normally; the arrow and +/− buttons remain available.
  3. Compare samples. Load several files together; stacked lanes share one genomic axis. Use the sample checkboxes to isolate or combine datasets.
  4. Loop color shows evidence. Soft coral (#FF7057) is stronger and blue is weaker. With an IGV export, color uses its column-8 −log10(q) score because exact q-values are no longer stored.
  5. Dashed arcs are clipped. One anchor is visible and the other lies beyond the current window; zoom out or use “Fit gene contacts” to reveal both.
  6. Click an arc or row. The selected loop receives a dark outline and its sample, anchors, and available statistics appear at right. Click it again, click a blank area, use “Clear selection,” or press Esc to remove the highlight.
  7. Add ChIP-seq or ATAC-seq context if available. Local narrowPeak and broadPeak files appear as peak blocks; local BigWig files appear as continuous signal with numeric values on the right. Each BigWig lane autoscales separately, so compare values only when the files use comparable units and normalization.
  8. The gene track is fetched from UCSC NCBI RefSeq for the visible region. A selected gene is highlighted.
  9. Be careful with interpretation. An overlapping loop is a candidate regulatory contact; confirming enhancer–gene function needs additional evidence.
BED/FitHiChIP coordinates are stored as 0-based, end-exclusive intervals. Labels in this interface are displayed as familiar 1-based genomic positions.
